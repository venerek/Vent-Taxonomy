🩸 ABG → Ventilator Adjustment

🫁 Ventilation — Acidosis
📋 PROBLEM ↑CO₂ / pH < 7.35
💡 GOAL Increase CO₂ clearance
🔧 LEVER Minute Ventilation (V̇E = VT × RR)
💊 ACTION ↑VT or ↑RR↑Pinsp (to ↑VT) or ↑RR
⚠️ Before ↑VT: check Pplat ≤ 30, ΔP < 15Monitor VT — may overdistend if compliance improves
🫁 Ventilation — Alkalosis
📋 PROBLEM ↓CO₂ / pH > 7.45
💡 GOAL Decrease CO₂ clearance
🔧 LEVER ↓ Minute Ventilation
💊 ACTION ↓VT or ↓RR↓Pinsp (to ↓VT) or ↓RR
⚠️ Ensure VT ≥ 6 mL/kg PBWEnsure VT ≥ 6 mL/kg PBW; monitor for apnea if ↓RR
💨 Oxygenation — Hypoxemia
📋 PROBLEM ↓PaO₂ / SpO₂ < 92%
💡 GOAL Improve oxygen delivery
🔧 LEVER FiO₂ + PEEP (alveolar recruitment)
💊 ACTION ↑FiO₂ first → then ↑PEEP
⚠️ Before ↑PEEP: consider hemodynamics — may ↓CO
💨 Oxygenation — Weaning
📋 PROBLEM Adequate O₂ on high FiO₂
💡 GOAL Reduce O₂ toxicity risk
🔧 LEVER Wean FiO₂ → then PEEP
💊 ACTION ↓FiO₂ to SpO₂ 92–96%, then ↓PEEP
⚠️ Wean FiO₂ before PEEP; monitor for derecruitment

💨 Oxygenation

FiO₂ 0.21–1.0
Target: SpO₂ 92–96%. "Should be set at the lowest value required to reach the oxygenation target" [3].
PEEP 0–22 cmH₂O
"Can maintain open recruited lung areas" [3]. Risks: overdistention, ↓cardiac output [3].

🌬️ Ventilation

Tidal Volume (VT) 6–8 mL/kg PBW
"Based on predicted body weight because lung size scales with PBW" [3]. In VC → set directly; In PC → dependent on mechanics [1].
Respiratory Rate 10–35/min
CMV rate = minimum; IMV rate = maximum [1].
Minute Ventilation (V̇E) VT × RR
Primary determinant of CO₂ clearance [3].

📈 Pressures

Peak Pressure (Ppeak) Highest during inspiration
R = (Ppeak−Pplat)/Flow [3].
Plateau Pressure (Pplat) ≤ 30 cmH₂O
"A measure of alveolar pressure, since resistance is zero at zero flow" [3].
Driving Pressure (ΔP) Pplat−PEEP < 15
"Driving pressure change was the variable that best predicted mortality" [3].
Pinsp Set above PEEP
Determines VT in PC modes [1].

🌊 Flow

Peak Inspiratory Flow ≥ 50 L/min
Set ≥ 50 L/min to avoid flow starvation [3].
Flow Waveform Square vs ramp
"Not all descending ramp waveforms are the same" [1].

🔧 Mechanics

Compliance (CRS) Normal ~50 mL/cmH₂O
CRS = VT/(Pplat − total PEEP) [3].
Elastance (E) Normal ~20 cmH₂O/L
Inverse of compliance. High elastance = stiff lungs [1].
Resistance (R) Normal < 10 cmH₂O/L/s
R = (Ppeak−Pplat)/Flow [3].
Time Constant (τ) R×C ~0.5s
Normal ~0.5s, ARDS ~0.4s, COPD ~1.3s. Need 3–5τ for complete exhalation [1].

🎛️ Trigger & Cycle

Trigger Sensitivity Flow 1–5 L/min or Pressure
"Flow triggering results in less effort than pressure triggering" [1].
Flow Cycle Threshold 30–70%
"High % in obstructive lung disease, low in restrictive" [3].

[1] Mireles-Cabodevila 2022 · [2] Chatburn 2014 · [3] Pham et al. 2017

Mode Classification
TAG = Control Variable – Breath Sequence – Targeting Scheme(s)

🫁 Foundations

M1 | A breath = one cycle of positive and negative flow

"A breath is one cycle of positive flow (inspiration) and negative flow (expiration)" [2].

M2 | Assisted ≠ Spontaneous

"A breath is assisted if the ventilator provides some or all of the work of breathing" [2].

🔗 Trigger & Cycle

Trigger = starts a breath | Cycle = stops a breath [2]

PatientVentilator
TriggerFlow/Pressure change (effort)Time (set rate interval)
CycleFlow decay (% of peak)Time or Volume

Spontaneous: Patient triggers AND patient cycles [2].

Mandatory: Ventilator triggers AND/OR ventilator cycles [2].

⚙️ Control Variable

Why This Matters:

Every breath requires pressure to overcome elastic load (lung stiffness) and resistive load (airway friction). The ventilator controls EITHER pressure OR volume/flow — but not both. [1]

Equation of Motion

Pressure(t) = E × Volume(t) + R × Flow(t)
■ E = elastance (elastic load) · ■ R = resistance (resistive load) [2]
PC — Pressure predetermined
✅ Set: Inspiratory pressure
📈 Dependent: Volume/Flow vary with E, R
💪 Effort: "Patient effort → more VT. Total work increases" [1].
VC — Volume AND Flow predetermined
✅ Set: VT AND flow
📈 Dependent: Pressure varies
💪 Effort: "Total work stays constant. As Pmus↑, Pvent↓. Patient can't get more volume" [1].

CMV | Continuous Mandatory Ventilation

Every breath is ventilator-cycled → every breath is mandatory [2]. Set rate = minimum [2].

⚠️ The patient CAN trigger — but every breath is still mandatory because the ventilator controls when inspiration STOPS (cycle) [2].

IMV | Intermittent Mandatory Ventilation

Spontaneous breaths possible between mandatory breaths [2]. Set rate = maximum [2].

Sub-typeDescription
IMV(1)Mandatory always at set rate. Spontaneous allowed between [1].
IMV(2)Spontaneous breaths suppress mandatory [1].
IMV(3)Spontaneous MV suppresses mandatory [1].
IMV(4)Dual targeting creates spontaneous breaths within apparent CMV [1].

CSV | Continuous Spontaneous Ventilation

All breaths patient-triggered AND patient-cycled [2].

VC-CSV is not possible: "Volume control implies ventilator cycling, and ventilator cycling makes every breath mandatory" [2].

⚠️ Clinical Context, Not Single Values

Readiness for rehabilitation should never be determined by an isolated number, lab value, or threshold. Decisions should integrate the patient's overall trajectory, hemodynamic stability, respiratory status, and response to current support.

🫀 Hemodynamic Trajectory

• Vasoactive trend — escalating vs. weaning/stable dose
• MAP stability over preceding hours, not a single snapshot
• Heart rate and rhythm trajectory

🫁 Respiratory Status

• FiO₂ and PEEP trajectory — stable/weaning vs. escalating
• SpO₂ trend and variability, not a single reading
• Ventilatory demand — is RR climbing? Air hunger?
• ABG trajectory vs. a single PaO₂ value

🩸 Perfusion & Oxygen Delivery

• Lactate trend — clearing vs. rising
• ScvO₂ trend if available
• End-organ perfusion signs — mentation, UOP, skin

📈 Overall Trajectory

• Improving, stable, or deteriorating?
• What changed in the last 4–12 hours?
• Sedation level and neurological engagement
• Team assessment and patient goals

How Modes Respond to Increasing Patient Effort

Adapted from Figure 7, Mireles-Cabodevila et al. 2022 [1]

● Green (flat): Pvent constant. Effort rewarded with more VT ✅
● Red (declining): Pvent↓ as Pmus↑. Work shifting occurs ❌

📊 Work Shifting by Mode

✅ PC-CMVs / PC-CSVs — Effort Rewarded
Pvent constant. As Pmus↑ → VT↑, Flow↑. Patient controls rate, flow, and Ti [1].
❌ VC-CMVs — Effort NOT Rewarded
"As Pmus↑, Pvent↓. Patient can't get more volume" — flow is locked, can't match demand [1].
Severe: "Patient is doing work on the ventilator" [1].
⚠️ PC-CMVa (PRVC) — Counterproductive
"As Pmus increases, Pvent will decrease in an attempt to maintain VT at target" — the vent actively reduces support as the patient works harder [1].

⏱️ Why Cycling Matters

Flow-Cycled (PC-CSVs)
"Rate of flow decay to cycle threshold determined by patient mechanics" [2]. Neural Ti ≈ Vent Ti → synchrony.
Time/Volume-Cycled (VC-CMVs)
Ventilator cycling — ends at preset regardless of patient. Mismatch → dyssynchrony.

💡 Late cycle: "patient's neural Ti shorter than imposed Ti" [1].

💡 Early cycle: "patient's neural Ti longer than imposed Ti" [1].

🔍 Dyssynchrony Recognition

"Patient-ventilator dyssynchrony...occurring in about one-third of patients" [3].

Trigger Phase

Normal: Immediate pressurization [1].
Late trigger: "Pressure drops below baseline > 100 ms before triggering" [1].
False trigger: "Non-Pmus signal triggers inspiration" [1].

Inspiration — Work Shifting

Severe: "Pressure drops below baseline (PEEP)" [1]. "Never appropriate" [1].

Cycle Phase

Late cycle: "Zero flow with concomitant increase in airway pressure" [1].
Early cycle: "Expiratory flow with evidence of inspiratory effort" [1].

Expiration

Normal: "Exponential decay of flow" [1].
Expiratory work: "Flow deformed in negative direction" [1].

📋 Optimization

These are considerations for optimizing the chances of a successful mobility session — not absolute requirements. Clinical judgment always applies.

Primary Considerations
  • 1
    FiO₂ — Oxygen Reserve
    Ensure adequate O₂ reserve. Consider transient ↑FiO₂ to provide margin during increased metabolic demand.
  • 2
    Mode — Flow on Demand
    Consider PC-CMVs or PC-CSVs if in VC-CMVs or PC-CMVa. Active patients benefit from modes that reward effort with flow [1].
  • 3
    PS Level — Support During Activity
    Consider temporarily ↑ pressure support to accommodate increased ventilatory demand.
Additional Considerations
  • •
    Trigger Sensitivity
    Verify flow trigger (1–3 L/min). "Flow triggering results in less effort" [1].
  • •
    Alarm Thresholds
    Adjust high VT and high V̇E alarms — prevents nuisance alarms that interrupt sessions.
  • •
    Circuit & Line Management
    Ensure adequate circuit length, secure lines, and plan repositioning logistics.
