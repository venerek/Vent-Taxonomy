💨 Oxygenation — Hypoxemia
📋 PROBLEM ↓PaO₂ / SpO₂ < 92%
💡 GOAL Improve oxygen delivery
🔧 LEVER FiO₂ + PEEP (alveolar recruitment)
💊 ACTION ↑FiO₂ first → then ↑PEEP
⚠️ Before ↑PEEP: consider hemodynamics — may ↓CO
💨 Oxygenation — Weaning
📋 PROBLEM Adequate O₂ on high FiO₂
💡 GOAL Reduce O₂ toxicity risk
🔧 LEVER Wean FiO₂ → then PEEP
💊 ACTION ↓FiO₂ to SpO₂ 92–96%, then ↓PEEP
⚠️ Wean FiO₂ before PEEP; monitor for derecruitment

💨 Oxygenation

FiO₂ 0.21–1.0
The proportion of oxygen in the inspired gas delivered to the patient.
Target: SpO₂ 92–96%. "Should be set at the lowest value required to reach the oxygenation target" [4]. "In patients with a large shunt, increasing FiO₂ has only minimal impact on arterial oxygenation" [4].
PEEP 0–22 cmH₂O
Positive pressure maintained in the airway at end-expiration to prevent alveolar collapse and improve oxygenation.
"Can maintain open recruited lung areas" [4]. Risks: "Can also lead to overdistention" and "decrease cardiac output" [4].

🌬️ Ventilation

Tidal Volume (VT) 6–8 mL/kg PBW
The volume of gas delivered to the patient during a single inspiration.
"Based on predicted body weight because lung size scales with PBW" [4]. In VC → set directly; In PC → dependent on mechanics [2].
Respiratory Rate 10–35/min
The number of breathing cycles per minute (set or total).
CMV rate = minimum; IMV rate = maximum [2].
Minute Ventilation (V̇E) VT × RR
The total volume of gas exhaled per minute; the primary determinant of CO₂ clearance.
"Pressures must be monitored when VT is set, and volumes must be monitored when pressure is set" [4].

📈 Pressures

Peak Pressure (Ppeak) Highest during inspiration
The maximum airway pressure during inspiration, reflecting both resistive and elastic loads.
In VC: R = (Ppeak−Pplat)/Flow [4].
Plateau Pressure (Pplat) ≤ 30 cmH₂O
The airway pressure during zero-flow conditions (end-inspiratory pause), reflecting alveolar distending pressure.
"A measure of alveolar pressure, since resistance is zero at zero flow" [4].
Driving Pressure (ΔP) Pplat−PEEP < 15
The pressure difference representing tidal stress applied to the lung.
"Equal to VT/CRS" [4]. "Driving pressure change was the variable that best predicted mortality" [4].
Pinsp Set above PEEP
The set inspiratory pressure above PEEP that drives gas flow into the lungs during pressure-controlled breaths.
Determines VT in PC modes [2].

🌊 Flow

Peak Inspiratory Flow ≥ 50 L/min
The maximum gas flow rate during inspiration.
In PC: peak flow = ΔP/R [2]. Set ≥ 50 L/min to avoid flow starvation [4].
Flow Waveform Square vs ramp
The shape of inspiratory flow over time, determined by mode and settings.
"Not all descending ramp waveforms are the same" [2].

🔧 Mechanics

Compliance (CRS) Normal ~50 mL/cmH₂O
The ease with which the respiratory system expands; the volume change per unit of pressure change.
Formula: CRS = VT/(Pplat − total PEEP) [4].
Elastance (E) Normal ~20 cmH₂O/L
The stiffness of the respiratory system; the pressure required to deliver a unit of volume. The inverse of compliance (E = 1/C).
High elastance = stiff lungs = low compliance [2].
Resistance (R) Normal < 10 cmH₂O/L/s
The opposition to gas flow through the airways.
Formula: R = (Ppeak−Pplat)/Flow [4].
Time Constant (τ) R×C ~0.5s
The time required for 63% of a pressure-driven volume change to occur.
Normal ~0.5s, ARDS ~0.4s, COPD ~1.3s [2]. Need 3–5τ for complete exhalation [2].

🎛️ Trigger & Cycle

Trigger Sensitivity Flow 1–5 L/min or Pressure
The threshold of patient effort required to initiate a ventilator-delivered breath.
"Flow triggering results in less effort than pressure triggering" [2].
Flow Cycle Threshold 30–70%
The percentage of peak inspiratory flow at which the ventilator terminates inspiration during pressure support.
"High % in obstructive lung disease, low in restrictive" [4].

TAG = Control Variable – Breath Sequence – Targeting Scheme(s)

🫁 Foundations

M1 | A breath = one cycle of positive and negative flow

"A breath is one cycle of positive flow (inspiration) and negative flow (expiration) defined in terms of the flow-time curve" [3].

M2 | Assisted ≠ Spontaneous

"A breath is assisted if the ventilator provides some or all of the work of breathing" [3]. A spontaneous breath may be assisted or unassisted.

🔗 Trigger & Cycle

Trigger = starts a breath | Cycle = stops a breath [3]

Spontaneous breath: Patient triggers AND patient cycles [3].

Mandatory breath: Ventilator triggers AND/OR ventilator cycles [3].

"Flow cycling is a form of patient cycling because the rate of flow decay to the cycle threshold is determined by patient mechanics" [3].

⚙️ Control Variable

Why This Matters:
Every breath requires pressure to overcome two forces: the stiffness of the lungs (elastic load) and the friction of air moving through tubes (resistive load). The equation of motion describes this relationship. Understanding it explains why different modes behave differently — and why some are better for active patients.
Equation of Motion

In plain terms: To deliver a breath, pressure must overcome:

  • Elastic load (E × Volume) — stiffness of lungs and chest wall
  • Resistive load (R × Flow) — friction through airways and ETT

The ventilator controls EITHER pressure OR volume/flow — but not both. Whichever it controls, the other depends on patient mechanics. [2]

Pressure(t) = E × Volume(t) + R × Flow(t)

E = elastance [3] · R = resistance [3]

PC — Pressure predetermined

✅ Set: Inspiratory pressure

📈 Dependent: Volume/Flow vary with E, R

👁️ Watch: Flow & Volume waveforms [2]

💪 Effort: "Patient effort → more VT. Total work increases" [2].

VC — Volume AND Flow predetermined

✅ Set: VT AND flow

📈 Dependent: Pressure varies

👁️ Watch: Pressure waveform [2]

💪 Effort: "Total work stays constant. As Pmus↑, Pvent↓. Patient can't get more volume" [2].

📊 Breath Sequence

CMV | Continuous Mandatory Ventilation

Every breath is ventilator-cycled → every breath is mandatory [3]. Set rate = minimum [3].

⚠️ Common misconception: "CMV means the patient can't trigger." WRONG.
The patient CAN trigger — but every breath is still mandatory because the ventilator controls when inspiration STOPS (cycle) [3].
IMV | Intermittent Mandatory Ventilation

Spontaneous breaths possible between mandatory breaths [3]. Set rate = maximum [3].

4 Sub-types [2]:

IMV(1)Mandatory always at set rate. Spontaneous allowed between [2].
IMV(2)Spontaneous breaths suppress mandatory [2].
IMV(3)Spontaneous MV suppresses mandatory [2].
IMV(4)Dual targeting creates spontaneous breaths within apparent CMV [2].
CSV | Continuous Spontaneous Ventilation
All breaths patient-triggered AND patient-cycled [3].
VC-CSV is not possible: "Volume control implies ventilator cycling, and ventilator cycling makes every breath mandatory" [3].

💪 Rehab & Synchrony

"There are only 3 goals of mechanical ventilation (safety, comfort, and liberation)" [2]. During rehab → comfort is primary (with safety maintained).

How Modes Respond to Increasing Patient Effort

Adapted from Figure 7, Mireles-Cabodevila et al. 2022 [2]

● Green (flat): PC-CMVs / PC-CSVs — Pvent constant. Patient effort rewarded with more VT ✅

● Red (declining): VC-CMVs / PC-CMVa (PRVC) — Pvent↓ as Pmus↑. Work shifting occurs ❌

"Work shifting occurs whenever the Pvent decreases in presence of Pmus" [2].

📊 Work Shifting by Mode

✅ PC-CMVs / PC-CSVs — Effort Rewarded

What happens: Pvent constant. As Pmus↑ → VT↑, Flow↑ [2].

Work: "Total work increases" but patient rewarded with more volume [2].

Why good for rehab: Flow on demand. Consistent support. PC-CSVs is flow-cycled = "patient cycling" [3].

❌ VC-CMVs — Effort NOT Rewarded

What happens: "As Pmus↑, Pvent↓. Patient can't get more volume" [2].

Severe: "Pvent decreases below baseline. Patient is doing work on the ventilator" [2]. "Severe work shifting is never appropriate" [2].

⚠️ Servo-U "Volume Control" ≠ VC-CMVs. Classified as VC-IMVd,d — dual targeting can switch from VC to PS within a breath [2].
⚠️ PC-CMVa (PRVC) — Vent Works AGAINST Effort

What happens: "As Pmus increases, Pvent will decrease in an attempt to maintain VT at target" [2].

Severe: "Patient may be breathing at PEEP level with little assistance and larger VT than target" [2]. "Severe work shifting in adaptive targeting should be addressed promptly" [2].

⏱️ Why Cycling Matters

Flow-Cycled (PC-CSVs)

Patient cycling — "rate of flow decay to cycle threshold determined by patient mechanics" [3]. Neural Ti ≈ Vent Ti → synchrony.

Time/Volume-Cycled (VC-CMVs)

Ventilator cycling — ends at preset regardless of patient. Mismatch → dyssynchrony.

💡 Late cycle: "patient's neural Ti shorter than imposed Ti" [2].

💡 Early cycle: "patient's neural Ti longer than imposed Ti" [2].

🔍 Dyssynchrony Recognition

"Patient-ventilator dyssynchrony...occurring in about one-third of patients" [4]. "Patients with greater numbers of dyssynchronies have poorer outcomes" [4].

Trigger Phase

Normal: Immediate pressurization [2].

Late trigger: "Pressure drops below baseline > 100 ms before triggering" [2].

False trigger: "Non-Pmus signal triggers inspiration" [2].

Inspiration — Work Shifting

Normal: No major Pmus deformation [2].

Work shifting: Pvent above baseline — may be acceptable [2].

Severe: "Pressure drops below baseline (PEEP)" [2]. "Never appropriate" [2].

Cycle Phase

Late cycle: "Zero flow with concomitant increase in airway pressure" [2].

Early cycle: "Expiratory flow with evidence of inspiratory effort" [2].

Expiration

Normal: "Exponential decay of flow" [2].

Expiratory work: "Flow deformed in negative direction" [2].

📋 Pre-Mobility Checklist

✅ Mode: Switch to PC-CMVs or PC-CSVs if in VC-CMVs or PC-CMVa. Patient needs flow on demand.
💡 Trigger: Ensure flow trigger (1–3 L/min). "Flow triggering results in less effort" [2].
💡 PS level: Consider temporarily ↑ for mobility session.
✅ FiO₂: Ensure adequate O₂ reserve. May need transient ↑FiO₂.
💡 Alarms: Adjust high VT and high V̇E alarms to accommodate increased volumes during exertion.
